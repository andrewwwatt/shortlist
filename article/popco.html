Popco: The inside story of SA's most surprising startup

By The Shortlist Team · 8 October 2026 · 8 min read

There is a moment every founder remembers. For the brother-and-sister team behind Popco, it came at a bottling plant in Paarl in March 2021, watching their fizzy creation roll off a third-party line they could barely afford. Two years later, the machine that makes their drink can't keep up with demand — and the phone hasn't stopped ringing since.

Popco wasn't supposed to happen. It started, like most improbable businesses, as a lockdown experiment: a family recipe, a WhatsApp group, a bet that South Africans were ready to drink something made here that wasn't a Coke variant or a boutique import priced like a mortgage.

The kitchen table origin

"Our gran made the syrup for us as kids," the founders recall. "When lockdown hit, we were sitting at home watching the big brands tell everyone to drink their way through the pandemic, and we thought — ours is better. Why shouldn't people be able to buy it?"

That question took 14 months and three rejected manufacturing contracts to answer. The first two bottlers told them the volumes were too small; the third quoted a price that would have made a can of Popco cost more than a can of imported Premium. They went back to the kitchen, bought a second-hand sealer, and kept going.

"Everyone told us the South African beverage market was closed. Retailers, distributors, even other small brands. Closed to everyone except the giants. We didn't believe them."

The break that changed everything

The turning point wasn't a deal — it was a bad one. A boutique retailer in Cape Town agreed to a trial, and a labeling mistake meant 1,200 cans shipped with the wrong logo. Rather than destroy them, the founders delivered anyway with a handwritten apology slip tucked into each box. The retailer reordered within a week. Word spread.

By 2024, Popco had signed its first national grocery listing. The interviews and the growth stories followed. But the founders are clear-eyed about what made the difference — not a secret formula, not marketing genius, but the willingness to be the smallest product in the biggest market, and to keep showing up.

What's next

The product is now on shelves from Cape Town to Polokwane, with export conversations underway. The founders are planning their first hire in a dedicated role they never expected to need: a quality control manager.

So what's the lesson? In a market where two giants control the fridges, the fridge doors are still wide open to anyone willing to walk through them. Popco walked. Slowly, badly funded, and occasionally mislabeled — but they walked.
