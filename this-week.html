Editor's Note

When the head of one of the world's biggest artificial intelligence companies asks his own industry to slow down, it's worth asking why. Is he genuinely frightened of what he's building, or would a pause simply suit the people already in front? Possibly a bit of both, which is what makes it interesting.

So we look at AI from three corners of the triangle this week: the bad news, the good news, and how it actually impacts you. Read all three and you'll be the best-informed person at the braai.

Truth really is stranger than fiction, this week. And yes, pretty much every week these days.

CGTN – Julie Scheier

South Africa's $100 million card game

The cards your children swapped at break have quietly become an economy. South Africa's trading card market — Pokémon, Magic: The Gathering, Yu-Gi-Oh! — is now put at around $100 million, driven by nostalgia, scarcity and a wave of overseas investors. A 1998 Pikachu card sold for $16.5 million in February, the most ever paid for a trading card.

EWN – Wendy Knowler

Think it's your bank calling? Just hang up

The call looks as if it's coming from your bank, and the person on the line knows your account details. That proves nothing, says consumer journalist Wendy Knowler: scammers buy leaked personal information and can fake the number on your screen. Her advice fits on a sticky note. Hang up, phone the number on the back of your card, and never share a one-time PIN or agree to a video call with someone who rang you.

Business Maverick – Stephen Grootes

The shopping mall refuses to die

We were told online shopping would finish off the mall. Stephen Grootes went looking for the body and found a crowd instead. Malls that give people a reason to turn up — a haircut, a coffee, a beauty counter — are doing well, while the ultra-cheap online sellers that were supposed to replace them are running into tighter rules and thinner profits. Shopping, like life, is going to stay both online and in person.

Over 60 and going places

South Africa's over-60s have nearly doubled in number since 2002, to 6.6 million — and they aren't staying home. Almost two-thirds of over-fifties planned to travel this year, and one trip abroad in five was a "bucket list" journey. Ghost Bites
Ghost Bites – Markets

Capitec was the JSE's biggest story this week, with profit and the dividend both up 19% for the six months to August. Business clients more than doubled to 686,000, its fintech arm (which now sells cellphones, putting a bank in competition with clothing retailers) earns more than its insurance business. The warning sign is that bad debts are creeping up. Clicks is paying R507 million to take control of ARC, the premium beauty chain whose Sandton City store is reportedly Africa's largest beauty shop. SPAR, still recovering from a botched computer system, warned that this year's results will be worse than last year's.

Gold Fields confirmed it made an offer for Australian miner Northern Star, but the target's board has said no for now. Gemfields wrote $125 million off the value of its Mozambique ruby mine, leaving it with an overall loss. And AngloGold Ashanti earned a public telling-off from the JSE for posting its results on its own website 24 minutes before telling the stock exchange.

The Witness

A "super El Niño" summer is on the way

El Niño is a natural warming of the Pacific Ocean that shifts weather around the world every few years, and this one is shaping up to be a monster: US forecasters put the odds at three in four that it will be the strongest since records began in 1950. For South Africa, the Weather Service expects a hotter summer than usual, with a growing chance of below-normal rain — so think heatwaves, fire risk and thirstier gardens. The reassuring part: we go in well stocked, with this year's maize harvest bigger than last year's. Planning, not panic.
