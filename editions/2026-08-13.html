🔖 Short List — Week of 11–14 August 2026

It's been a week of rare celestial wonder and quiet economic green shoots. Europe had a total solar eclipse — Spain's first in over a century — while back home, Ramaphosa flipped the switch on our new digital travel pass, and the PSL gave us a table-topping Kaizer Chiefs we haven't seen in years. Unemployment ticked up again, but there's a surprising amount to feel good about.

1. 👩‍💼 Why women investors quietly outperform men

Financial Mail · 12 Aug 2026

New research confirms what many quietly suspected: women investors beat men on returns — not through superior stock-picking skill, but because they trade less, take fewer risks, and actually stick to their plans. It's a powerful reminder that patience, not adrenaline, wins the long game. Next time someone mansplains the markets at a braai, you'll have data.

→ Read on Financial Mail

2. 🌑 Total solar eclipse spellbinds Europe

EWN / AFP · 12 Aug 2026

Millions of Europeans gazed skyward on Wednesday as the moon's shadow swept from Arctic Russia across Greenland and Iceland, before carving a diagonal line through Spain. The total eclipse lasted less than two minutes, but "eclipse chasers" had been planning for years. Spain expects the cosmic event to deliver €347 million in tourism spending and 446,000 extra visitors. The next one crosses southern Spain in August 2027 — book now.

→ Read on EWN

3. 🎓 Advtech expects 13–18% earnings growth

Moneyweb · 11 Aug 2026

South Africa's largest private education group, Advtech (owners of Crawford, Trinityhouse, and Varsity College), says it expects interim earnings to grow between 13% and 18%. In a country desperate for good education news, a private provider showing sustained growth is worth noting. Meanwhile, tile retailer Italtile is bracing for a tough year — proof that not every number is going up.

→ Read on Moneyweb

4. 🪼 Jellyfish knock one-fifth of France's nuclear capacity offline

The Witness / AFP · 12 Aug 2026

Sometimes the most delightful news stories come from the unlikeliest places. A massive swarm of jellyfish drifted into the cooling water intake at a French nuclear plant, forcing a shutdown that knocked 20% of the country's nuclear capacity offline. Power grid engineers versus gelatinous sea creatures — and the jellyfish won this round. France has seen this before, but never at this scale.

→ Read on The Witness

5. 🏉 Springbok Impi star brings inspiration to Clarendon Primary

The Witness · 12 Aug 2026

KZN-born Springbok Impi Sibusiso Kotelo visited Clarendon Primary School in Pietermaritzburg this week, and the school erupted. Cheers, songs, and a gymnasium full of children who suddenly believe the green and gold is within reach. "Representation matters" isn't just a slogan — it's what happens when a local kid makes it and comes home to show the next generation the path.

→ Read on The Witness

6. ✈️ Ramaphosa launches South Africa's digital travel pass

Business Day · 13 Aug 2026

South Africa's Electronic Travel Authorisation system is live. President Ramaphosa formally launched the digital travel pass this week — a system that lets visitors apply online before flying, aiming to speed up entry, tighten border security, and boost tourism. Think of it as South Africa's version of the American ESTA or European ETIAS. Finally, one less queue at OR Tambo.

→ Read on Business Day

7. 📡 Frogfoot debuts pay-as-you-go fibre in Tshwane

Business Day · 13 Aug 2026

Frogfoot Networks has launched a contract-free, pay-as-you-go fibre service in Lotus Gardens, Tshwane, using aerial fibre technology strung along existing poles. No credit checks, no lock-in contracts — just internet you can buy in chunks, like airtime. It's a model that could genuinely change the connectivity game for lower-income suburbs. Watch this space.

→ Read on Business Day

8. ⚽ Kaizer Chiefs are top of the league. No, really.

Explain · 13 Aug 2026

Kaizer Chiefs are top of the Betway Premiership table with a perfect start, and Orlando Pirates dropped their first points in a 1-1 draw with AmaZulu. Is this finally the Chiefs rebuild fans have been waiting for? Coach Fernando Da Cruz has Amakhosi playing with a belief that's been missing for years. It's early days, but the Naturena faithful are daring to dream again.
