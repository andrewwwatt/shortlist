Why women investors outperform men

New research confirms what a lot of people quietly suspected: women's portfolios beat men's over time – not through sharper stock-picking, but because they trade less, take fewer impulsive risks, and actually stick to the plan they made. It's a tidy piece of evidence that patience beats adrenaline in investing, and a good one to have on hand next time someone at a braai starts confidently explaining the market to you.

Young women top the property charts

For the first time on record, young women buying property solo (34%) now outnumber both mixed-gender couples (31%) and men buying alone (33%) among under-35 buyers – up from just 20% in 2005. It's less a change in taste than a change in timeline: women are reaching financial independence, and getting there before anyone waits for a partner to catch up.

Business Day

SA's new digital border, explained

The government's new Electronic Travel Authorisation lets eligible travellers apply online – a selfie, a passport scan, some light biometric checking – and get approved within 24 hours, no embassy visit required. More than 216,000 people used it in the pilot ahead of SA's G20 presidency, with over 6,000 fraudulent applications already caught and blocked. Ramaphosa is framing it as an economic intervention rather than an immigration tweak: every visa headache, the logic goes, is one more reason an investor or tourist picks somewhere else.

Business Day

Fibre you buy like airtime

Frogfoot Networks has launched a contract-free, pay-as-you-go fibre service in Lotus Gardens, Tshwane, strung along existing poles with no credit checks and no lock-in – just connectivity you top up when you can afford it. It's one suburb so far, but if the model holds, it's the kind of unglamorous infrastructure story that could genuinely change who gets to be online in this country.

Ghost Bites – Markets

Ghost Bites – Markets

This week's JSE mining news came with the usual mix of triumph and whiplash. Southern Palladium jumped roughly 20% after securing the mining right for its Bengwenyama PGM-chrome project in Limpopo – a real milestone, undercut only by the project sitting inside a subsidiary called "Miracle Upon Miracle Investments." Merafe has done even better, up 35% since January on strong chrome ore prices and an Eskom lifeline keeping its smelters running. Gold Fields wasn't so lucky – despite releasing a trading statement indicating a 70–90% increase in headline earnings per share, its share price is down 12% this year, proof that mining shares chase today's commodity price. Meanwhile Impala Platinum delivered proper whiplash: earnings are set to hit R26.52 a share, up from just 82 cents the year before – a swing of more than 30 times – but its share price is down 22% this year.

Elsewhere, Spur just learned what an unsigned handshake deal can cost: a near-decade fight with a ribs supplier has ended in a R129.5 million provision, denting full-year earnings by over a third, even though the restaurant business itself was quietly having a good year – not the type of rib deal you expect at the chain. And a genuinely striking number from Shoprite: full-year earnings are set to grow by up to 14.7%, while its delivery arm Sixty60 grew revenue to R25.5 billion, up 34.5% – now bigger than Takealot's online retail business. The Checkers app may quietly be winning the online shopping war.

Everyone's Talking About

The week South Africa turned white

A cold front colliding with a cut-off low buried the high country from the Drakensberg to the Cape fold mountains last weekend – the N3 closed at Van Reenen, the N2 shut near Kokstad, and Johannesburg didn't crack 4°C. SAWS forecaster Tokelo Chiloane called it a classic "double whammy" system, and the photos backed him up: snow on Franschhoek and Swartberg passes, on Joburg rooftops in Bedfordview and Oakdene, on the road up to Lesotho's Katse Dam.

Chasing it this weekend? Most of the low-lying snow has already melted, so don't expect a repeat at sea level. Afriski, on the Lesotho border, is the one place still holding a base – both lifts running, pistes open – and stays open through end of August, though no fresh snow is forecast (Tiffindell, SA's other ski resort, has been closed since 2020 and never reopened). For scenery over skiing, the Swartberg and Franschhoek passes are worth the drive if you want to see where it fell, mud and all.
