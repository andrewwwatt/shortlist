Editor’s Note

This week, we're following the money – from Airbnbs in Cape Town (are they still worth it?) to the sale of the Carlton Centre (fancy owning what was once Africa's tallest building?), and even the growing side hustle of renting your car out to an Uber or Bolt driver.

We're also taking you off the beaten track a bit – to roads less travelled, as the popular pursuit of Caminos takes off in South Africa, and the coffee market continues to grow on the back of conversation.

Did you know that the coffee market was never about the coffee at all? "It was caffeine and an excuse to sit down together, stay awake, and talk," says Dominique Olivier. And because of this, coffee shops became the birthplace of insurrection.

. Maybe there's a rebellion left in us somewhere?

If you're all out of talk, how about a walk? Pick one of the dozen or so Camino trails that have quietly sprung up across the Cape, the Karoo and the Garden Route – multi-day walks with your luggage moved ahead and nothing required of you but the next step.

Maybe this is one of the reasons the tourism market is on the up and up? (Another story covered below, of course.)

South Africa's tourists are back – but will they stay?

Ghost Bites – Markets

Ghost Bites – Markets

Two of the week's best results told opposite stories on the share price. OUTsurance jumped 9% after posting HEPS (headline earnings per share, the market's preferred profit yardstick) growth of between 21% and 26%, with the South African business up between 40% and 46% and three-year shareholders sitting on a 147% total return. Harmony Gold posted an even bigger HEPS jump of 87%, with the dividend more than quadrupling from 155c to 750c, yet the stock fell almost 5%, the worst performer on the JSE that day, because next year's gold and copper production guidance came in lower than expected. Good numbers, it turns out, aren't always good enough.

There was drama of a different kind at Northam Platinum, which confirmed it has received an unsolicited approach from a major PGM producer about a possible asset level or full corporate deal. At a market cap of R114 billion, that's a serious appetite test for the platinum sector, and Northam says it's open to hearing other offers too. Nothing is signed yet, so treat this as one to watch rather than one to bank on.

Away from the mines, Bidcorp is a reminder of how far a South African company can travel. Only 14% of group revenue now comes from the Emerging Markets segment, which lumps South Africa in with the Middle East and Latin America. Europe contributes 38% of revenue, the UK another 29%, and Australasia the remaining 19%. It's best understood as online grocery shopping, but for restaurants and hotels, built through years of careful bolt-on deals into something genuinely global.

Less fun was Italtile, the tile and bathroom retailer many of us have renovated with, currently battling a soft local market and a struggling Australian arm. New CEO Brandon Wood has inherited a proper renovation job, not just a regrout, with HEPS down 9.4% for the year.

KwaZulu-Natal – A weekend of books under the Yellowwoods

Now in its 17th year, the Midlands Literary Festival takes over The Old Mushroom Farm in Howick this Saturday and Sunday – readings, talks and Q&As with authors including John van der Ruit and Ashwin Desai, plus former Witness editor John Conyngham on his first novel in almost three decades. Founder Darryl Earl David built it into one of KZN's biggest book events from a single weekend in a Howick café.

Western Cape – A flower show that only happens once every 20 years

The 2025 wildfires that scorched the Overberg triggered something remarkable: a fynbos "superbloom" around Walker Bay near Hermanus that only happens once every 15–20 years. Over 200 flower species, several found nowhere else on Earth, are in full bloom right now and will stay that way into late September – worth the drive from Cape Town on its own.

Everyone’s Talking About

Everyone's Talking About

Walking it out the Camino way

A Camino, at its simplest, just means "a way" – the term comes from the Camino de Santiago, the centuries-old pilgrim route across northern Spain to the shrine of St James, walked in daily stages over a week or more. No religious conviction required, just a start date and decent boots – and the format is spreading fast: a record 530,000-odd pilgrims completed the Spanish original in 2025, the third record year running, while Camino-style routes have sprung up on nearly every continent since. Much like the running boom of the last decade, walking tends to have a moment whenever the world feels like a lot – it's slow, cheap, and asks nothing of you beyond the next step.

Depending on how loosely you define a Camino, South Africa now has anywhere from four to well over a dozen. The original was the Cape Camino, launched in 2013 and grown into a 700km network of self-guided sections across the Peninsula, Winelands and West Coast. Since then, others have followed – the Tankwa Camino through the Karoo, the Plett and Hermanus Caminos on the Garden Route and in the Overberg, the Sungazer Pilgrimage in the Free State, plus assorted routes through the Klein Karoo, Namaqualand and Baviaanskloof. They all follow the same formula: multi-day, self-paced, slackpacking, with your luggage moved ahead so you arrive each night to a bed, not a backpack. No spiritual prerequisite required, just a few days and a decent pair of shoes.
