🔖 Short List — Week of 3–6 August 2026

Another week of heavy headlines: the Madlanga Commission hearings continued to draw crowds, a judgment loomed in the Phala Phala farm theft case, and petrol prices pleasantly surprised by dropping 52 cents — though diesel users weren't so lucky. Meanwhile, the All Blacks touched down for the start of their SA tour, and Johannesburg acquired a new mayoral candidate brave enough to actually want the job. Here's what you might have missed while the news cycle did its thing.

1. 🚬 Why cigarettes are suddenly 'cool' again in pop culture

EWN (Primedia) · Kabous Le Roux · 5 August 2026

Cigarette imagery is creeping back into film, fashion and social media — and it's not an accident. Pop culture specialist Motheo Moleko unpacks the unlikely revival, from runway styling to TikTok aesthetics, and why a habit we spent decades trying to kill is suddenly glamorous again. A genuinely fascinating dinner-party topic that sits right at the intersection of marketing, nostalgia and public health.

→ Read on EWN

2. 🐙 The world's first octopus farm has been scrapped

EWN (Primedia) · Vicky Stark · 3 August 2026

A Spanish seafood giant has abandoned its controversial plan to build the world's first commercial octopus farm, after years of pushback from scientists and activists who argued these astonishingly intelligent creatures simply can't be farmed humanely. Marine biologist Maryke Musson explains why octopuses — curious, tool-using escape artists — were never going to take well to captivity. A rare good-news environmental story with a touch of "I told you so."

→ Read on EWN

3. 💰 Avoiding lifestyle creep: how to make a raise actually count

EWN (Primedia) · Paula Luckhoff · 4 August 2026

Warren Ingram, one of SA's most sensible personal finance voices, has practical advice for anyone who's ever looked at a bigger paycheque and wondered where it all went. His core message is refreshingly simple: enjoy your life, but pick the things that truly bring you joy rather than upgrading everything by default. Smart without being preachy — exactly the kind of financial advice you can actually use.

→ Read on EWN

4. 🍷 Michelin's wine rankings stumble out of the gate

Business Day · Michael Fridjhon · 5 August 2026

The famous Michelin Guide has just issued its first ever formal wine ratings — and according to SA's most respected wine commentator, they've already blown it. Fridjhon argues that in focusing on Burgundy, Michelin broke its own sacred rule of neutrality by bending the rankings to protect a famous producer's reputation. For anyone who loves wine (or just enjoys a good schadenfreude story about an institution getting it wrong), this is a cracking read.

→ Read on Business Day

5. 🚙 The new Land Cruiser has a load-shedding survival feature

Business Day · Motoring Staff · 5 August 2026

Toyota has launched the hybrid Land Cruiser 300 in South Africa, and while the 341kW V6 hybrid is impressive, the most practical detail is something else entirely: a built-in 1,500-watt AC power outlet under the centre console. Yes, this R2-million-plus beast can double as your backup generator during load-shedding. It'll do 10 litres per 100km, too — not bad for a 2.7-tonne SUV.

→ Read on Business Day

6. 🏉 Rugby's money problem: why the All Blacks tour ends in Baltimore

Business Day · Gavin Rich · 3 August 2026

As the Springboks prepare to host the All Blacks in one of rugby's great rivalries, Gavin Rich points out something absurd: the series finale won't be in South Africa — it'll be in Baltimore, USA, chasing American dollars. Rich argues rugby is starting to mirror football's worst impulses, where commerce trumps tradition and fans get priced out of their own sport. A thoughtful, slightly melancholic read for anyone who cares about the game beyond the scoreboard.

→ Read on Business Day

7. 🏎️ 2,500 classics descend on Zwartkops for Cars in the Park

The Citizen (Caxton) · Shaun Holland · 4 August 2026

More than 2,500 classic cars — and 12,000 enthusiasts — gathered at Zwartkops Raceway for the 44th edition of Cars in the Park, hosted by the Pretoria Old Motor Club (which turns 60 this year). From lovingly restored Beetles to gleaming American muscle, the photos alone are worth your time. Pure, uncomplicated joy on four wheels.

→ Read on The Citizen

8. 🏛️ Why a rising political star is leaving Parliament for Joburg city hall

Explain · Prashalan Govender · 5 August 2026

Build One South Africa's deputy leader Nobuntu Hlazo-Webster is making an unusual career move: swapping a national parliamentary seat for a shot at Johannesburg's mayoral chain — arguably the toughest job in local government. In a warm, wide-ranging interview, she explains why fixing potholes and billing systems feels more meaningful than speeches in the National Assembly. A genuinely interesting profile of a politician doing the opposite of climbing the ladder.

→ Read on Explain

9. 🏗️ Why property developers aren't nearly as rich as you think

Moneyweb · Brent Geddes · 5 August 2026

Everyone assumes property developers are printing money — but Brent Geddes, who has spent a decade funding them, breaks down the brutal numbers. Between the regulatory maze, 54-month project timelines, and a dozen different consultants all incentivised to say "better check" rather than "go ahead," the margins are far thinner than most people imagine. A myth-busting read that might make you slightly more sympathetic the next time you see a construction site.

→ Read on Moneyweb
