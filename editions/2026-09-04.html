Business Day

You can bring China into Africa, but you can't take the South African out of the Hilux

New-vehicle sales hit 57,898 units in August, up 11.4% on last year, and Chinese brands are making real inroads – Chery, GWM and Jetour now all sit in the top 10, outselling established names like Kia. But the podium hasn't budged: Toyota's Hilux remains SA's best-seller, the Ford Ranger is close behind, and the VW Polo Vivo tops the passenger-car charts. Toyota alone still outsold its nearest rival, Suzuki, more than two to one.

DStv is about to look very different – and there's finally a sports-only option

Leaked pricing suggests MultiChoice is preparing a full overhaul of its streaming packages from 17 September: a R99 Starter tier, R299 Select, a standalone R399 Sports package with 115 channels (Premier League, UEFA, T20 cricket, local rugby), and a R500 Movies & Series option. Ghost Bites – Markets

Ghost Bites – Markets

Shoprite just posted the kind of results that make you want to buy shares out of sheer spite for anyone else in retail. Sales grew 7.2%, trading profit was up 8.4%, and gross margin actually improved to 24.5%, which is remarkable for a value retailer. The real engine is Sixty60, up 34.7% to R25.5 billion in sales and now live in 997 stores. And just to remind everyone who's boss, they're picking up Vida e Caffè and its more than 400 stores, plus a majority stake in R&A Cellular. When you're this good at retail, apparently you get to buy the coffee shop too.

Meanwhile, two of the country's best-known retailers had rough weeks for very different reasons. Woolworths announced it's stepping back from Fashion to focus on Food, Beauty and Home, but the market wasn't cheering, sending the share price back toward its 52-week low. Turns out competing head-on with Checkers on food is no small ask, especially when Shoprite's gross margin in that category is now beating Woolworths Food's own. The Foschini Group had a rough week of its own, with sales growth of just 0.2% and its Australian business shrinking 4.7%, prompting plans to close dozens of stores over the next few years.

Sasol shareholders have had a rough few years, so here's a stat worth sitting with: the total return on the share over the past five years is just 3.3%. Not per year. In total. But refining margins more than doubled this year as Middle East tensions pushed up fuel prices, helping the share price climb more than 90% since January. Even the market's most patience-testing stocks can turn around fast when the world cooperates.

And finally, evidence that family splits don't always end amicably. Rainbow Chicken, spun out of RCL Foods in 2024, delivered a 130% earnings jump and a special dividend on top of the ordinary one. RCL Foods, left holding the sugar and pet food divisions, had a year to forget, complete with a salmonella scare that would put anyone off their dog's dinner. Somebody got the good half of the settlement.

What’s On This Weekend

What's On This Week

Not lucky enough to have tickets to Saturday's decider? Or one of the few South Africans who doesn't watch rugby? Either way, here's what else is on this week.

Gauteng – The Cape Winelands come to Sandton

Babylonstoren has set up its first-ever shopping centre pop-up in Hyde Park Corner's Centre Court, running until 14 September. Babylonstoren in Bloom is a spring installation modelled on the farm's famous Puff Adder walkway, with garden displays to wander through and the farm's bath and body ranges, home fragrance, linen, honey and wine on hand to sample and buy. No flight to Paarl required.

KwaZulu-Natal – Eat your way around the world

Three days, 75-plus vendors and enough food trucks to make a decision genuinely difficult – the Durban Street Food Festival takes over The Pavilion from 4–6 September. Mafikizolo and Muzi headline the music line-up, and Saturday's Boks decider will be screened at a dedicated fan park, so you can eat your way through the weekend without missing kick-off.

Western Cape – Cape Town's creative underground gathers

Side Quest Socials brings geeks, nerds and pop-culture fans together at Fish Hoek Civic Centre on Saturday. Expect a vendor market of handmade fan-franchise art and collectables, a trading-card corner, and a workshop called Roll & Reveal, hosted by Geek Culture South Africa in support of local niche-fandom makers. R30 entry, free for under-10s.
