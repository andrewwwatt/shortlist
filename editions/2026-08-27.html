🔖 The Short List — Week of 24–27 August 2026

It was the week we said goodbye to Dolly Parton — and then immediately discovered she'd left us a secret song to be opened in 2045, because of course she did. Meanwhile, tourists are flooding back to South Africa in their best numbers in ages, a conservationist is fighting to save the world's most famous psychedelic toad, and the Cape's answer to Burgundy is still very much on top. Some weeks are just more interesting than others.

1. 🎵 Dolly Parton's secret song, buried until 2045

EWN / AFP · 27 August 2026

Dolly Parton died this week at 80, but she had a plan to keep the music coming: she buried a secret song called "My Place in History" in a time capsule at her Dollywood resort, not to be opened until late 2045 — around the 100th anniversary of her birth. She also recorded vocals and click tracks for hundreds of unrecorded songs, so collaborators can keep producing "new" Dolly music long after she's gone. As she once put it: "I'm one of those people that believe in being prepared." A legend, to the very last note.

2. 💼 Henry Ford, Walt Disney and Colonel Sanders all went broke first

Financial Mail · Antoinette Steyn · 27 August 2026

Before Ford, Disney, KFC's Colonel Sanders and Bill Gates changed the world, their first ventures collapsed — Disney even lived on dog food for a while after his studio went bankrupt. FM talks to South African entrepreneurs and business-school deans about why failure is only useful if you actually learn from it ("failure teaches nothing by itself"), and how founders like Hiten Keshave rebuilt after losing absolutely everything. A genuinely inspiring read — and a reminder that a flop isn't the end, it's often just the tuition.

→ Read on Financial Mail

3. 🐸 The man saving the toads people smoke to get high

Business Day · Kimon de Greef · 25 August 2026

Meet Robert Villa, a self-described "freelance naturalist" from Arizona who became the world's foremost defender of Sonoran Desert toads — chicken-sized amphibians whose venom contains 5-MeO-DMT, the "God molecule" now driving a psychedelic poaching boom. This extract from Kimon de Greef's book Ego Trip is a wild ride through Yaqui mythology, underground drug newsletters and a man who printed T-shirts reading "Protect the Sonoran Desert toad. Please trip responsibly." You'll never look at a toad the same way again.

→ Read on Business Day

4. ✈️ Tourists are coming back to South Africa in a big way

Business Day · Stella Mapenzauswa · 27 August 2026

International tourist arrivals to South Africa surged 17% in July, and government is now pinning its hopes on tourism as an engine for job creation and economic recovery. After years of being one of the economy's quiet disappointments, the sector's numbers are finally moving in the right direction — good news for everyone from game lodges to township tour operators.

→ Read on Business Day

5. 🍷 The Cape's answer to Burgundy is still on top

Business Day · Michael Fridjhon · 26 August 2026

Wine writer Michael Fridjhon toasts Hamilton Russell Vineyards — "Cape royalty" that helped shape the modern South African wine industry — after sampling the latest releases. The verdict? The 2025 pinot noir is comfortably the best of the modern era, the R180 Southern Right sauvignon blanc is "the best way to spend R180" if you love the grape, and the R1,000 Ashbourne pinotage is worth every cent. Consider your next dinner-party bottle sorted.

→ Read on Business Day

6. 💊 A cheaper Ozempic has landed in South Africa

Explain · Calay Blaauw · 24 August 2026

Everyone's heard of Ozempic, the diabetes drug that became the world's favourite off-label weight-loss secret. South Africa has just launched Extensior — a cheaper authorised copy of the exact same active ingredient, made by the same company in the same doses, but at a lower price. Explain breaks down what it is, who it's actually for (type 2 diabetes, not a lifestyle shortcut), and how to dodge the dodgy knock-offs flooding social media.

→ Read on Explain

7. 🏭 A former JPMorgan banker came home to make things again

Explain · Verashni Pillay · 25 August 2026

Irshaad Kathrada spent nine years at JPMorgan before the pull of home became too strong. Now he runs the Localisation Support Fund, trying to rebuild South Africa's shattered manufacturing base — starting with 81 million garments a year that could be made locally instead of imported from China. It's an uplifting, jargon-free conversation about why making things matters, and why a single factory job creates four more. The kind of story that makes you quietly proud to be South African.

→ Read on Explain

8. 🎬 Storm is coming to Joburg (and so is Rick Sanchez)

EWN · 26 August 2026

Comic Con Africa has added Alison Sealy-Smith — the iconic voice of Storm in X-Men: The Animated Series and X-Men '97 — to its 2026 lineup, alongside Rick and Morty's Rick Sanchez (Ian Cardoni) and actor Richard T. Jones. The convention runs 24–27 September at Nasrec, and if you have a Marvel fan in the house, you now have the perfect excuse to dust off a costume.
