🔖 Short List — Week of 27–30 July 2026

Johannesburg's luxury property market is quietly staging a comeback while Cape Town's CBD just broke a development record. Between the Madlanga Commission drama, a Commonwealth Games medal haul in Glasgow, and Banyana Banyana kicking off their WAFCON campaign in Morocco, it's been a week where South Africans had plenty of reasons to look up from the doom-scrolling. Here's your dinner-party-ready reading list.

1. 🎵 10 thoughts I had while Tyla's A\*POP ruled my day

Explain · Ratiloe Zingeni · 27 July 2026

What happens when you let South Africa's biggest pop star soundtrack your entire day, from breakfast doubts to midnight hot-tub energy? This delightful track-by-track diary follows one writer as Tyla's new album A\*POP drags her through an emotional crisis, restores her confidence, convinces her she has plans, and eventually deposits her in an imaginary hot tub at midnight. It's less a music review and more a reminder that the right album at the right time can turn an ordinary Tuesday into a music video.

→ Read on Explain

2. ⚽ Banyana Banyana kicks off WAFCON campaign – and fans can expect this talented team to go far

Explain · Kamogelo Ndlovu · 27 July 2026

If you thought you could pack away the soccer jersey after Bafana's World Cup run, think again. South Africa's women's team just started their WAFCON campaign in Morocco, carrying the double burden of defending their 2022 title and qualifying for next year's World Cup in Brazil. With Thembi Kgatlana and Hildah "The Breadwinner" Magaia leading the attack, there's every reason to believe they can bring it home.

→ Read on Explain

3. 🏠 Joburg's grand property revival

Currency News · Joan Muller · 27 July 2026

After years of being Cape Town's unfashionable cousin, Joburg's luxury property market is suddenly the one people are talking about at dinner parties. Multi-year sales records are falling in Sandhurst, Hyde Park and Waterfall — including a R75m contemporary number in Sandhurst and a R89m offer pending at Waterfall Equestrian Estate. The reason? Simple: you get twice the house for half the price compared to the Western Cape, and wealthy buyers are doing the maths.

→ Read on Currency News

4. 🏗️ Cape Town CBD property development breaks R12.8bn record

EWN / Primedia · Camray Clarke · 30 July 2026

Not to be outdone, Cape Town's inner city just posted a 41% jump in property development value to R12.8 billion, driven largely by residential towers and international hotel groups betting big on the Mother City. The CCID's Rob Kane noted that the City is also spending R5.5 billion on MyCiTi bus and rail upgrades — the kind of infrastructure commitment that makes long-term investors sleep easier.

→ Read on EWN

5. 🚙 MINI teases rugged Countryman Edition for 2026 debut

Business Day · Phuti Mpyane · 29 July 2026

MINI is taking its compact crossover off-road — literally. The new Countryman Edition was recently spotted tackling gravel tracks and switchbacks in the Rocky Mountains during pre-production testing, and the company says it's the most adventure-ready MINI yet. If you can't wait until the global October reveal, MINI South Africa has quietly released 100 local "Rugged Edition" Countrymans with all-terrain tyres and roof-box mountings. Your safari-lodge driveway awaits.

→ Read on Business Day

6. ✈️ Sun, surf and safari: why SA tourism is still winning

Business Day · Stella Mapenzauswa · 30 July 2026

Despite a spike in air travel costs driven by the Middle East conflict, nearly 1.1 million foreign travellers entered South Africa in June — up 4.3% year on year. Turkish Airlines is bumping its direct flights to 20 per week from October, and Latam Airlines just launched a direct São Paulo–Cape Town route. The takeaway? People still want to come here, and the infrastructure is quietly keeping up.

→ Read on Business Day

7. 🏨 Southern Sun starts R250m overhaul of its biggest hotel

Moneyweb · Suren Naidoo · 29 July 2026

Durban's iconic Elangeni-Maharani — Southern Sun's largest hotel, sitting right on the Golden Mile — is getting its biggest makeover in decades. The R250 million refurb follows the group securing a multi-decade lease from eThekwini Municipality, suggesting someone is betting that Durban's hospitality scene has a lot more life in it than the headlines would have you believe.

→ Read on Moneyweb

8. 🤦 When marketing misses the market

Ghost Mail · Dominique Olivier · 26 July 2026

Starbucks South Korea ran a "Tank Day" promotion on the anniversary of the Gwangju Massacre — a pro-democracy protest crushed by the military using, yes, tanks. The CEO was fired, all 2,100 stores closed for mandatory sensitivity training, and $260 million in pre-paid card balances nearly walked out the door. Dominique Olivier uses this spectacular own-goal to explore a whole genre of corporate marketing disasters, from Lady Gaga's breast-milk ice cream feud to the two Scottish brewers who legally changed their names to Elvis to keep their IPA. It's the most entertaining business story you'll read this week.

→ Read on Ghost Mail
